Constructive mathematics / from the ground up

Mathematics on
a finite machine.

Build the computer. Make its limits visible.
Ask what mathematics we can construct inside it.

A mathematical symbol is small. What it asks us to do may not be. To construct a value, run a program, or check a proof, we need somewhere to store the work and time to carry it out.

We begin below the notation, with a switch. From switches we build logic, memory, and a tiny computer. Then we ask how definitions and proofs should account for the computer’s limits.

Read straight through, or use the contents to jump. In bit grids, green means 1; pale means 0. Each figure labels what its colors mean.

This first part is executable. The proposed route from bounded constructions to analysis is a research direction, with its open obligations stated explicitly.
